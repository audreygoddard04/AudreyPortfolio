# KELTNER editorial linking rules

Each published article has a parent-category link and an “Explore more [section]” link to its category hub. The canonical categories are Style, Places, Motors, Travel, The Home, Music, and Culture; older Estates/Cars/Motoring references resolve to the current names.

## Before publishing

1. Add one or more precise **Topic clusters** in Studio. Reuse tags rather than creating near-duplicates: `timeless-wardrobe`, `winter-wardrobe`, `grand-hotels`, `european-hotels`, `historic-houses`, `architecture-books`.
2. Choose 2–4 **Related articles** when the published archive supports it. Prioritize the same topic; cross sections only where a reader has a clear reason to continue. Leave a short list rather than adding unrelated filler.
3. Choose one **Relevant commercial guide**, such as a buying guide, booking guide, or sourcebook. Mark those destination articles **Commercial guide** so relevant automatic recommendations can also find them. Do not invent a guide or add a booking link just to meet a quota.
4. Optionally select a **Broader pillar article**. The section hub is always linked even when no pillar article exists.
5. Add contextual inline links where they help the reader. Review 2–3 older related articles for useful forward links in their prose.

## How recommendations work

Only currently published, dated articles returned by the public query are eligible. Drafts, future posts, removed references, self-links and duplicates are excluded. Curated references rank first, followed by reverse references, shared topic tags, and matching canonical categories. At most four related stories appear. Commercial and pillar recommendations use separate slots. No unrelated story is inserted to fill a quota.

Selecting a related article automatically makes the newer article eligible for the older article's recommendations too, after the normal cache refresh. This supports bidirectional discovery, but does not replace the editorial review for contextual links in the body. If more than four candidates compete, the relevance ranking determines which appear.

The two launch articles have a curated connection: “The Things Worth Keeping” introduces the publication's enduring-design theme; “Does Beauty Increase The Value Of Real Estate?” develops it through architecture. Explicit Studio related-article selections override this initial recommendation.
